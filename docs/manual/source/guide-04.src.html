Moving Around the Plan

ToDo this

PanDrag an empty part of the plan.

Move a free trussDrag it (half-foot steps; ends snap to other ends within 1.5 ft; hold Alt for no snapping). Bolted trusses and blocks cannot be dragged.

The hint bar at the bottom of the plan is a reminder of these.

What the Plan Shows

[[img legend.png|The key (legend) in Workload mode.|55]]
  • Trusses - bars to scale, coloured by the current colour mode, each labelled with its name and length (turned so it never reads upside down).
  • Corner blocks - dark squares (red if more trusses are bolted to one than it has ways).
  • Bolted connections - small diamonds; red when the bolted parts do not match (different truss families).
  • Hoists - a ring with cross-hairs, labelled with the high hook static load and, in italics under it, the high hook dynamic load. The ring is amber when the hoist is high (or its dynamic load is over capacity) and red when it is overloaded or slack. A dashed ring is a hoist hung below a truss; a filled dot is a dead hang.
  • Loads - small triangles with their weight (lighter for a mirrored twin). Untick Loads in the view toolbar to hide them.
  • Labels - untick Labels to hide names and numbers for a cleaner picture.
  • X 0 / Y 0 - the origin lines.

Colour Modes

The buttons in the view toolbar choose what the truss colours mean. The key (bottom right of the plan) changes with them.

ModeColours each truss by…
WorkloadIts highest workload - of its span, cantilever, moment, shear and deflection checks and the hoists on it: green under 80%, amber 80-100%, red over 100%. The default.
Hot spotsLocal workload along its length, from green through amber to red, peaking under the loads that work it hardest. A glow marks the peak of any truss at 80% or more. Use it to find where on a truss the problem is.
DeflectionIts worst span's sag against its limit (green under 80%; amber from 80% or past the rig default; red past the maker's published limit; grey if it has no span to check). The deflected shape is drawn exaggerated beside each truss - a sag is drawn to its right-hand side. Hover a shape for its largest sag.
HoistsThe workload of the busiest hoist on it (grey: no hoists).
Pass/failGreen if everything passes, red if anything fails.

The 3D View

Click 3D in the top bar to see the rig in 3D, with the hoists' chains rising to their hook points. It is for looking and presenting: you edit in the plan and the panels. Colour modes work the same (in Deflection mode the shapes are drawn above the trusses).

Allowances

Weight the drawing does not show. Allowances load the trusses and hoists like any other weight.

SettingWhat it does
Cable allowanceCable weight per foot (lb/ft, kg/m) on every truss, added to its UDL. A truss can have its own in its panel (0 = none).
Load factorsA multiplier for each load category - Lighting, Audio, Video, Scenic, Rigging hardware, Other / not set. For example 1.1 on Lighting to allow for clamps, safeties and colour frames not listed. Set each load's category in its panel or the Loads grid. The loads keep the weights you typed; the calculation uses the factored weights.

Truss Checks

SettingWhat it doesDefault
Repetitive-use factorThe ANSI repetitive-use rule multiplies table capacities by 0.85 unless the maker's data already includes it. per truss data follows each truss's data (the generic Universal trusses use 0.75); or force always 0.85 or none (1.0).per truss data
Truss self weightOn: each truss's own weight is added to the load on each cantilever, and included in the moment/shear check (and added back into the allowables, as the tables are loads on top of the truss's weight). Off: left out of both.on

Deflection

Limit where the maker publishes none - span ÷ this number (default 160, i.e. L/160). Past this default a span gets a warning. Where a maker publishes a limit (Tomcat L/100; some JTE sheets L/160) that limit always applies, and past it the truss is Overloaded (the maker's allowable loads are set by it). The list under the setting names every truss with a published limit; each name opens the maker's data sheet.

Plan

Center on 0,0 moves the whole rig so the middle of its footprint is at X 0, Y 0. Bolted trusses and corner blocks move with the trusses they hang from. No result changes, and Undo moves it back.

The Calculation Sheet

The calc sheet is a printable record of the rig's calculation, written for independent review of the software's work: every input, formula, table look-up and result is set out so it can be checked by hand or in another program. It shows only what the app has calculated; opening it changes nothing.

Opening, Filling In and Printing

  1. Click Calc sheet (top right, or the add button in step 4).
  2. Fill in the boxes along the top: Project / event, Venue / location, Prepared by, Checked by and Notes. They are printed on the sheet and saved with the rig.
  3. Click Print / save PDF. In your browser's print dialog, choose a printer, or Save as PDF / Microsoft Print to PDF to make a PDF file. Choose the same paper size as the sheet (Rig settings › Calc sheet paper).
  4. Click Close or press Esc to go back to the rig. (While the sheet is open, the rig cannot be edited.)

Files, Export and Sharing

Saving a Rig as a File

File › Save downloads the rig as rig name.rig.json to your browser's download folder (your browser may ask where to save it). The file contains the whole rig - trusses, blocks, loads, hoists, rig settings, calc sheet fields - plus the custom trusses, corner blocks, hoists and fixtures you have added, and the version of the app that saved it. It is a small text file: back it up, email it, keep it with the show paperwork.

Opening a Rig File

File › Open… and choose a .rig.json file. It replaces the rig on screen (save that first). If the file cannot be read, the app says why and leaves the current rig alone.

Opening a file also replaces your custom trusses, hoists and fixtures

In version {{VER}} the custom database saved inside the file replaces the one in this browser - it is not merged. Custom entries you added since that file was saved (or that were never in it) disappear from the browser. They are still inside any rig file saved while they existed. Before opening someone else's file, save your own rig (which saves your custom entries with it).

Files from other versions

Rig files record the version that made them. A newer version of the app opens older files. Results may differ between versions where the calculation has changed - the version history (File › About) says which versions changed results. Opening a file made by a newer version in an older one may not work.

Sharing a Rig

Send the .rig.json file. The other person opens it with File › Open… in their copy of the app. For people who do not have the app, send the calc sheet as a PDF, a picture of the plan, or the hoist table.

Using the App on Another Computer

Copy the app file and your .rig.json files. Custom trusses, corner blocks, hoists and fixtures travel inside every rig file you save, so opening your most recent rig file on the new computer brings them along.

Calculators and Databases

The Calculators menu opens standalone tools in place of the rig. Click ‹ Back to the rig to return. They do not change the rig.

Circular Truss

For a ring of truss hung from evenly spaced pick points. Choose the truss (manufacturer, model), the number of pick points and the diameter. It shows the maximum allowable load on a span (between two picks), the span (chord) against the maximum span, the angle of arc, the cantilever distance (how far the arc bows out from the straight chord) and its ratio to the span, the base load and the 20%, 35%, 70% and 100% load bands, with a drawing. Match original spreadsheet reproduces the original workbook's method (its 5-ft column look-up, no derate; imperial trusses only). Not every truss is made in circular form - check with the manufacturer - and loads must be balanced so the ring does not tip.

[[img tool-circular.jpg|The Circular truss calculator.|80]]

Simple UDL

A quick table for a uniformly distributed load across a truss on 2 to 10 equally spaced points with no cantilevers: type the total load and read the load on each point. By default it uses the published rounded coefficients; tick Use exact beam-engine coefficients for the exact values.

[[img tool-udl.jpg|The Simple UDL table.|80]]

Fixture Weights

The fixture library: 269 fixtures from the original workbook, with weights and (where known) clamp weights. Search by name, or filter by manufacturer. To add your own fixture, fill in Manufacturer, Fixture, weight and (optionally) clamp weight at the bottom and click Add fixture. Your fixtures appear in every fixture search, are kept in this browser, and are saved inside your rig files.

[[img tool-fixtures.jpg|The fixture library.|80]]

Databases

Calculators › Databases lists every truss and corner block the app knows - 277 trusses from 17 makers (60 from the manufacturers' own current tables, the rest from the Truss Load Analyzer workbooks; 101 with native metric tables) and 51 corner blocks from Christie Lites and James Thomas Engineering - plus anything you have added.

[[img tool-databases.jpg|The Databases page.|100]]
  • Search by any part of a name, code or spec; filter to Trusses or Corner blocks, and by maker.
  • Click a Family to see a truss together with the corner blocks that fit it (click the family chip to clear it).
  • The Source column links to the maker's load table (trusses) or product page (corner blocks), or says workbook for data from Hall & Sogoian's workbook. Hover a row for where its numbers come from.
  • Corner block weights marked n/a are not published - enter your own on the block in the rig.

Adding Your Own Truss

Open Add your own truss. Enter the manufacturer, model, weight per foot, maximum span, and the maximum UDL (total) and centre point load (CPL) - these are used for every span up to the maximum. For values that change with span, paste one number per foot (1 ft to 100 ft; in metric, one per metre) into the optional UDL and CPL boxes, separated by commas. Tick Data already includes the repetitive-use factor if the maker's table already includes it. Click Add truss. It is saved in the units you entered it in and is marked custom in the pickers.

Adding Your Own Corner Block

Open Add your own corner block: maker, truss family, name, what it fits (e.g. 12x12), number of ways, weight and size. Click Add block.

Custom Chain Hoists

At the bottom of the page: brand, model, capacity label (e.g. 1 Ton), capacity, speed, body weight and chain weight per foot. Click Add hoist. A hoist with no speed uses the rig's default dynamic factor.

Custom entries can be deleted with the × on their row. They are kept in this browser and saved inside your rig files.

Custom data is your responsibility

Enter manufacturer figures exactly as published, and note whether the table already includes the repetitive-use factor. The app cannot check custom data.
