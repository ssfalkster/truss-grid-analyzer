Before You Start

Truss Grid Analyzer works out the load on every chain hoist and checks every truss in an entertainment truss rig, from a single truss on two hoists up to a grid of trusses bolted together at corner blocks. You draw the trusses, put the loads on them, place the hoists, and it shows you, as you type, whether each hoist and each truss is within the manufacturer's limits.

A calculation aid, not an engineering approval

Check the results against the manufacturer's current data, and have a qualified person review every rig before it flies. You are responsible for checking the results before you use them to make rigging decisions.

Indeterminate rigs (ANSI E1.6-3)

ANSI E1.6-3 (Selection and Use of Serially Manufactured Chain Hoists in the Entertainment Industry) calls a rig indeterminate when it hangs from more than two hoists in a straight line, or more than three hoists not in a line - a truss on three hoists, or almost any grid or box. On an indeterminate rig the load on each hoist depends on how stiff the trusses, the rigging and the structure above are, and load can shift between hoists as they run. The standard (section 7.1) accepts a load calculation as a way to reduce the risk of overload only when every weight is known and accounted for, the calculation method is accurate, and any load shifting will not be enough to cause an overload. This app's whole-rig analysis is built for these rigs, but it is an estimate: allow for load shifting, level the hoists carefully, and verify loads (for example with load cells) where the margin is small.

Open the App

  1. The whole app is one file, index.html (on GitHub it is dist/index.html). Save it anywhere on your computer.
  2. Double-click it. It opens in your web browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari). No install, no account, no internet connection needed, and nothing is sent anywhere.
  3. The first time, it shows an example box so you can look around. The version number is next to the name, top left (this guide: v{{VER}}).
Your work is kept in this browser only

Every change is saved automatically inside the browser on this computer. Clearing the browser's data, or opening the file in a different browser, gives you a different (or empty) workspace. Use File › Save to keep a rig as a .rig.json file you can back up, email and open again.

Your First Rig

This walk-through builds a 30 ft truss hung on two 1-ton hoists, with six moving lights. It takes about ten minutes. Every number below is what the app shows for version {{VER}}.

Step 1 - Structure: Add the Truss

  1. Choose File › New rig and click OK. (A new rig replaces the one on screen. Undo brings it back until you close the page, but save anything you want to keep first.)
  2. Click the rig name at the top (Untitled rig) and type My first rig.
  3. Click + Truss. A 20 ft truss called Truss 1 appears on the plan and in the side panel.
  4. In the side panel, under Truss, pick the Manufacturer and Model you use. This example keeps the default, JTE 12x12 Plated.
  5. In Truss pieces (ft), type 30 and press Enter. The truss is now 30 ft long.
[[img qs-1-truss.jpg|Step 1. One 30 ft truss. It shows Not solved and the status says 1 to check because it has no hoists yet - that is expected.|100]]
Typing lengths

Type decimal feet (12.5) or feet and inches (12-6, 12'6", 12' 6 1/2", 12 ft 6 in). In metric (Rig settings › Units) type metres (3.8), 380 cm or 3800 mm.

Step 2 - Loads: Hang Six Lights

A new truss measures positions from its centerline (CL): -12 is 12 ft before the middle, +12 is 12 ft after it.

  1. Click step 2 Loads (or press 2).
  2. Click the truss on the plan. In the side panel, open Loads. In the box that says Add: fixture or weight, type MAC Viper Wash @ -12 and press Enter. The app finds the fixture in its library (72.8 lb) and places it 12 ft left of the centerline.
  3. Do the same with MAC Viper Wash @ -7 and MAC Viper Wash @ -2.
  4. Click Mirror all. Each light gets a twin on the other side of the centerline (at +12, +7 and +2), so there are now six.
[[img qs-2-loads.jpg|Step 2. Three loads, each mirrored (×2 in the grid). The grid under the plan lists every load; you can type there too.|100]]

For a load that is not in the library, type its weight instead: 120 lb @ 6. You can also type straight into the Loads grid: the Item / fixture cell searches the library as you type.

Step 3 - Hoists: Hang It Up

  1. Click step 3 Hoists (or press 3).
  2. In the grid under the plan, find the row + hoist on Truss 1 (type its position) and click its At (ft) cell. Type -13 and press Enter. A hoist appears 13 ft left of the centerline (2 ft in from the end).
  3. The cursor is now back on the + hoist row, so just type 13 and press Enter for the second hoist.
  4. The Hoist column shows the model (the first one is a 1-ton CM Lodestar with 20 ft of chain). To change it, click the cell and type part of a name - lode 1t, prostar, 2 ton - then pick from the list. Set the chain length in the Chain column.
[[img qs-3-hoists.jpg|Step 3. Two hoists. On the plan each one shows its high hook static load (434) and, in italics, its dynamic load (549).|100]]
Other ways to add hoists

Select the truss, click + Hoist above the plan (it adds one in the middle of the selected truss - with no truss selected it adds a new truss instead), then drag it along the truss on the plan - it moves in 1-inch steps and shows its position as you drag.

Step 4 - Results: Read Them

Click step 4 Results (or press 4, or click the status at the top).

[[img qs-4-results.jpg|Step 4. All OK. Each hoist carries 433.7 lb static and 549.4 lb dynamic, 20% of its 2,200 lb capacity.|100]]

Reading the Results

Colours and Statuses

Colour / statusMeaningWhat to do
OKUnder 80% of what the manufacturer allows.Nothing.
High80-100%. Within limits but little margin.Consider it; check your weights are right.
OverloadedOver 100% of a limit (a hoist's capacity, a span or cantilever table, bending moment, shear, or the maker's deflection limit).Must be fixed: move or lighten loads, add or move hoists, or use a stronger truss or hoist.
Too longA span is longer than the truss table allows, or a cantilever is longer than a quarter of the maximum span.Add a support or shorten the overhang.
SlackThe load would push a hoist up. A chain can only pull, so it carries nothing and the rig is solved without it.Move the hoist or the loads; the other hoists now carry more.
UnstableA truss or assembly is not held up properly (for example one hoist only, or all hoists in a line) and would tip.Add hoists. Do not use its numbers.
Not solvedThe truss has no supports yet, or depends on something missing.Add hoists or bolt it to another truss.

Where to Look

  • The status at the top sums up the whole rig. Click it to jump to the results.
  • Step 4 Results has three tabs: Trusses (every check for every truss), Hoists (every hoist's loads), and Warnings (everything the app wants you to look at). Click a row to select that truss or hoist.
  • Click a truss and open Checks in the side panel: a drawing of the truss with its supports and loads, where it works hardest, its reactions, shear and moment diagrams, deflection, and a bar for each check.
  • Click a hoist to see its low hook, high hook static and high hook dynamic loads, and a table of where its load comes from.
  • Check rig (click an empty part of the plan to show the rig panel) lists likely input mistakes: a load with no weight, two hoists at one point, bolted ends that don't meet, too few hoists.
Level sensitivity

On short, stiff spans a hoist running just 1/4" high or low can change its load by hundreds of pounds. The app warns when that is more than 10% of a hoist's capacity - the example box shows this warning. Level those hoists carefully.

Building a Grid or a Box

Trusses join at corner blocks. A corner block belongs to one truss (add it in that truss's Corner blocks section: + Corner block at start, + Corner block at end, or at a distance). Then select another truss, click Bolt to… and click the corner block on the plan. The bolted truss moves to the block and follows it from then on. File › Load example box shows a complete box built this way. See the User Guide, chapter Step 1 - Structure, for the details.
