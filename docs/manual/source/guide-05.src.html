How the app calculates

You do not need this chapter to use the app, but it helps to know what the numbers mean and where they come from. The full technical description is in File › About, method & version history and on the calc sheet (section 2, Basis of calculation).

From loads to hoist loads

  1. Every truss is a beam carrying its point loads, its own weight (spread along it), its UDL and cable allowance, and anything passed in from trusses bolted to it or its corner blocks. Load factors (by category) and the Add % are applied as set in Rig settings.
  2. The whole-rig analysis solves all the trusses together as beams meeting at the corner blocks - a direct-stiffness (finite element) analysis of the grid, often called a grillage. Trusses bend (through their chords), shear (through their diagonals) and twist; their stiffness is estimated from the manufacturer's tables unless the truss data gives real chord and diagonal sizes. Hoists are rigid supports at one level unless you give them a stiffness.
  3. Because nobody publishes the stiffness of the joint through a corner block, the rig is solved with the joints hinged (the bolt passes vertical force only), semi-rigid (springs of 1, 4 and 16 × EI/L) and rigid (bending and twisting pass through). Each hoist's result is the largest of them, and each truss is checked with the joint model that loads it hardest (named in its Checked with).
  4. A chain can only pull. If a hoist's reaction comes out negative, it is marked Slack, taken out, and the rig solved again. If that leaves a truss or assembly without enough support, it is Unstable.
  5. Each hoist's high hook static load = its low hook load (the reaction) + Add % + the hoist body + chain (length × weight per foot) + hardware. The high hook dynamic load = static × the dynamic factor.

If the whole-rig analysis cannot run (a very large rig, or a load-path loop) the app falls back to the original workbook's load-path method - each truss solved on its own as a continuous beam (Clapeyron's three-moment equation), passing its reactions down the chain of bolted trusses to the hoists - and warns that it may under-estimate hoists in a grid.

Truss checks

CheckRule
SpanThe span length is rounded up to the next whole foot (metre, for metric tables) and that row of the maker's table is read. Capacity = the table's centre point load (CPL) × the repetitive-use factor k, reduced by the share of the uniform-load capacity already used by any UDL. The point loads on the span must not exceed it, and the span must not be longer than the table's maximum.
CantileverNo longer than a quarter of the maximum span, and its load (point loads + UDL share + self weight, by default) no more than the CPL for a span four times the cantilever's length, × k.
Moment and shearThe bending moment and shear all along the truss (from the whole-rig analysis) against an allowable estimated from the tables: every table entry is a load the maker says the truss carries, so it shows a moment and a shear the truss can take. These are estimates - use the maker's published moment and shear where you have them.
DeflectionEach span's sag, measured from the straight line between its two supports, against the maker's published limit (past it: Overloaded) or the rig default L/160 (past it: a warning). Cantilever tips are shown, not checked.
Repetitive useCapacities × 0.85 (ANSI) unless the data already includes it; the generic Universal trusses use 0.75.

Hoist checks

A hoist's status compares its static high hook load with its rated capacity (as the original workbook does). Its dynamic load is flagged separately when it exceeds capacity. A dead hang is checked against its WLL.

Checked against

The app's methods are checked in its test suite against the original workbook's results, the worked examples in Delbert L. Hall's Rigging Math Made Simple, an independent 3D frame analysis (PyNite) and Production Assist's calculation of the same grid.

Warnings and messages

Warnings appear in step 4's Warnings tab, in the Trusses tab, in the banner, on the calc sheet and (for input mistakes) in Check rig. Most name the truss (and hoist) they are about - click the warning to select it. Notes are shown in grey and are not counted as warnings. In the messages below, <Truss> stands for the truss's name.

Troubleshooting and questions

My rig has disappeared.

The app keeps the rig in this browser on this computer (see Where your work is kept). It is not there if you are in another browser, a private window, on another computer, or if the browser's data was cleared - or if you started a new rig or opened another file. Open your last saved .rig.json with File › Open….

I pressed New rig by mistake. Can I undo it?

No - in version {{VER}} Undo cannot bring back the rig replaced by New rig, Open or Load example box, even though the New rig message says it can. Open your last saved file.

A truss says "Not solved".

It has no support yet (add hoists or bolt it to a corner block), or it depends on something that is missing or on a loop of bolted trusses. The warning says which.

I can't drag a truss.

Bolted trusses (and corner blocks) are placed by their block: move the truss that owns the block, or Unbolt it first.

"Bolt to…" is greyed out.

There are no corner blocks on other trusses yet. Add a corner block to the truss you want to bolt to (its Corner blocks section).

The truss bolted to the wrong side or the wrong end.

Use Flip side or Bolt other end in its Placement section, or Unbolt and bolt again with the options set (its start / its end meets the block, extends north/south/east/west).

A load or hoist is at the wrong position.

Check its From: the same number means a different place from the start, the centerline or the end. On a centerline-measured truss, positions before the middle are negative.

Clicking + Hoist added a truss.

The add button adds to the selected truss; with nothing selected it adds a truss. Undo, select the truss, and click again.

The hoist loads don't add up to what I expect.

Open the hoist and look at Where the load comes from. In a grid, hoists share load through the trusses, so a hoist carries some weight from neighbouring trusses (or has a little lifted off it). The joint model used is named underneath. Compare analysis methods in step 3 shows how much the joint assumption matters.

Why are hoist loads so different when I move a hoist a little?

Short, stiff spans are level-sensitive. See Hoist levels. Giving hoists a stiffness in Rig settings makes the analysis more realistic.

My load cells don't match the app.

Check you are comparing like with like (Load cells read in Rig settings: high hook or low hook), that every weight is entered (cable, clamps), and compare assembly totals rather than single hoists - levels and stiffness move load between hoists.

A manufacturer link doesn't open.

Links go to the maker's website and need an internet connection. Makers sometimes move their documents.

The app looks different from this guide.

You are probably running a later version. This guide is for version {{VER}}. See File › About for what has changed.

Where do I report a problem?

Save the rig as a file and send it, with a description, to the app's author (see Credits).

Glossary

TermMeaning
Add %An optional extra percentage on the load at every hoist (Rig settings).
Allowable (moment, shear)The bending moment or shear a truss may carry, estimated in this app from the maker's load tables.
AssemblyA group of trusses joined together by bolts, stacking or corner blocks.
BoltedJoined to another truss through a corner block (or bolted end to a truss). A bolted truss is positioned by its block.
Calc sheetThe printable calculation for independent checking.
CantileverThe part of a truss past its outermost support. Max length = max span ÷ 4.
CarrierA truss that other hoists hang below.
Centerline (CL)The middle of a truss line; positions measured from it are negative toward the start.
Check rigThe list of likely input mistakes, in the rig panel.
ClampThe weight of the fixture's clamp, added to the load.
Corner blockA block trusses bolt to at right angles; part of one truss line in the app.
CPLCentre point load: the maker's allowable single load in the middle of a span.
Dead hangA support of wire rope and hardware with no hoist.
DeflectionHow far a span sags, measured from the line between its supports; limit given as span/ratio (e.g. L/160).
Derate / repetitive-use factorThe multiplier on table capacities for repeated use (0.85 per ANSI unless included).
Dynamic factor (DLF)Multiplier for loads while a hoist moves: speed (fpm) ÷ 60 + 1.
FingerprintA short code on the calc sheet that changes whenever any input changes.
GrillageThe engineering name for the whole-rig analysis of a grid of beams.
Hangs fromWhat holds a truss up (its hoists, blocks and trusses), or where a hoist hangs from (the structure, or below a truss).
High hook loadLow hook load + Add % + hoist, chain and hardware: what the structure above carries. Static and dynamic.
Hinged / semi-rigid / rigid jointsThe three ways the whole-rig analysis models the joints through corner blocks; the worst is used.
Hot spotsA colour mode showing where along each truss it works hardest.
Level offsetA designed height difference for one hoist (+ higher).
Level sensitivityThe change in a hoist's load if it runs 1/4" high or low.
LineA truss line: truss pieces plus corner blocks, end to end.
Load factorA multiplier on loads of one category (Rig settings).
Load-path methodThe original workbook's method, used as a fallback when the whole-rig analysis cannot run.
Low hook loadWhat the truss hangs on a hoist's hook (the reaction, R).
MFGTruss data from the manufacturer's own current load table.
MirrorA load's twin at the mirror position about the centerline (loads); a mirrored copy (hoists).
PiecesThe truss sections in a line, not counting corner blocks.
SlackA hoist whose chain would go slack because the load pushes it up.
SpanThe part of a truss between two supports.
Stack / clampA truss resting on top of (or clamped under) another where they cross.
TrussIn the app: one straight truss line.
UDLUniformly distributed load: a weight spread evenly along a truss (typed as a total).
UnstableNot held up by enough supports; would tip.
Whole-rig analysisThe stiffness analysis of the whole rig that gives the hoist loads and the forces the checks use.
WorkbookHall & Sogoian's Truss Load Analyzer - EOT Excel workbook, the original program.
WorkloadLoad as a percentage of what is allowed.
WLLWorking load limit.
